# History of Liankou

Liankou was cut in 1978 from a coastal port and a strip of bonded ground, as a special zone in the opening. The interior would remain under the plan. The fenced coast would deal, invite outside and overseas Chinese capital, and retain what it earned, so long as the exception stayed inside the wall and could still be described as socialism developing the forces of production.

The [Regulation Office](regulation-office.md) was the experiment's organ: recursive reporting from dock, mill, dormitory, and customs, gathered into an operations room. Early links were telex. Fifth-generation expert systems replaced the first staff as the holiday renewed. Growth figures became the political defense of the fence.

Construction followed the extensions. Decks were poured to meet the contract after the one being signed. Brigade halls were scraped and replated for contract mills. Joint-venture atria went up in Shangcheng while dormitory slabs were framed into the gaps below the [service band](geography.md). By the mid-1980s Liankou was no longer a port with a planning aid attached. It was a continuous city, engineered at the crown and occupied on arrival everywhere else.

The interior still cites Liankou as a contained reform. The zone committee still cites the forces of production. The lower city remains, in the filings, transitional.
