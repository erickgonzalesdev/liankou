# Expert systems in Liankou

**Expert systems in Liankou** are a class of knowledge-based computer systems used by the [Regulation Office](regulation-office.md) to score and allocate resources inside the [Liankou Special Zone](liankou.md). They apply rule bases, maintained by knowledge engineers, to live reports from docks, mills, dormitories, and customs desks, and return the result as a weighted token. The systems are the machine layer of [Project Tiaojie](project-tiaojie.md).

The zone committee classifies them as a modernization of planning under the doctrine of regulation by the market. Operators refer to the installation as the operations room. In the lower stack the outputs are called the weights.

## Background

Expert systems of the late 1970s and early 1980s encoded a specialist’s decision procedure as a set of rules and applied those rules with an inference engine. The Japanese Fifth Generation Computer Systems project (1982–1992) proposed a further step: parallel inference machines and logic-programming environments intended to handle knowledge rather than only numerical calculation. Most such programs remained at the stage of prototypes and research literature.

Liankou adopted the methods without adopting an external institute. The first Regulation Office links were telex. Clerks reduced dock and mill reports by hand. Fifth-generation machines replaced that staff in the early 1980s. Inference boards and process tools were supplied, until its seizure, by [Lixin Precision](lixin.md).

## Architecture

A Liankou expert system consists of a knowledge base and an inference engine. The knowledge base holds domain rules: weights for shifts, bunks, clearances, payroll names, and berth assignments. The engine applies the rules to the current feed. Systems are domain-specific. A customs engine does not assign bunks. A labor engine does not clear a hull. The Regulation Office performs the reduction across domains and issues the allocation.

Live knowledge bases are held in climate-controlled halls in the upper city. The lower stack receives only the token. Appeals terminate at the service band. Sensor inputs do not.

## Deployment

The systems score credit, piece-rate, bunk assignment, water, customs clearance, and export risk. A changed weight can reassign a shift or fail a dock token between reporting cycles. The zone committee treats clearance time as the public measure of the installation. Knowledge-base maintenance is an administrative procedure in the upper city. Rule updates are not published below the service band.

Retired cathode tubes from the halls are rebuilt into boards in the lower city. The display characteristics match the operations room. The systems’ authority does not extend to those boards.

## Bootleg weights

Copies of rule sets, sold as a means to read or alter an allocation, circulate in the lower stack. They rarely execute on the live systems. Possession is not systematically prosecuted. The live knowledge bases are not distributed below the band.

## Political status

The Zone Committee files the halls under regulation by the market inside the plan. Official statements do not claim abolition of the commodity form. They claim its use for export clearance and labor allocation. Altering a weight is an upper-city procedure. Being subject to a weight is the ordinary condition of a temporary permit.
